---

### BC 2026 Rent Control at 2.3%
The allowable annual rent increase is capped at 2.3% for 2026. Landlords must provide 3 months' notice and can increase rent only once per 12 months. Bad-faith eviction protections escalated significantly—tenants can now claim up to 13 months' rent in damages if a landlord uses personal-use evictions to circumvent vacancy control.

**Practical Implication:** Renewal timing matters. Landlords should plan renewals carefully; rent-controlled increases offer predictability but limit upside. Vacancy control remains in effect—rent resets to the unit, not the tenant.

### CMHC Expansion Opens New Lending Options
New rules effective December 2024 now allow insured mortgages up to $1.5 million (from $1 million). First-time buyers can access 30-year amortizations on new construction. Factory-built homes now eligible for insured financing. Multi-unit property pricing simplified and modernized.

**Impact on Investment:** Lower down payments and longer amortizations increase buyer pool, especially for first-time and modular/factory-built properties. Expect continued supply growth in purpose-built rental (25,855 units registered in BC in 2025—40% above prior year).

## 2. BC RENTAL MARKET OVERVIEW

### Vacancy Crisis Easing—Rents Falling
BC rental market saw dramatic shifts in 2025–2026:

- **Province-wide:** Average rent down 5.9% ($2,336/month for purpose-built). Vancouver apartment rents fell 5.3% ($2,679/month). Victoria declined 14.4% year-over-year.
- **Vacancy:** Metro Vancouver reached 3.7%—highest since 1988. Healthy for renters; challenging for landlords.
- **Key Driver:** 25,855 purpose-built rental units added in BC in 2025; federal immigration cuts reduced temporary resident admissions by 43%.

### Market Segmentation
- **High-end units (3+ bedroom):** Substantial vacancy, rent pressure downward.
- **Affordable units (1–2 bedroom):** Still tight; vacancy 1–2%. Landlords retain pricing power in this segment.

### Regional Notes
- **Metro Vancouver:** Softest market. 3.7% vacancy signaling landlord caution.
- **Victoria:** 14.4% year-over-year decline—accelerating softness.
- **Nanaimo:** Defying provincial trend—rents continuing to climb, construction underway, demand holding up (likely student/professional inflow).

**Outlook:** Rental supply growth will persist through 2026. Expect continued downward pressure on mid-to-high rent segments. Affordable units remain resilient.

## 5. LANDLORD & INVESTOR ACTION NOTES

1. **Renew Fixed-Rate Leases Now** – With 2.3% annual cap locked in for 2026, lock in renewals before late summer to capture maximum increases. Each month delayed = 0.19% lost allowable increment.

2. **Refinancing Watch** – Mortgage renewals at current 4.09%–4.25% rates are manageable but tighten cash flow. If holding investment property, evaluate: refinance now or hold to maturity? Rates appear stable through end-2026.

3. **Nanaimo vs. Lower Mainland Positioning** – If diversifying portfolio, Nanaimo rental still shows rent growth and stable vacancy. Lower Mainland facing multi-year supply overhang; prioritize Nanaimo for new acquisitions if targeting cash-on-cash returns.

4. **Bad-Faith Eviction Risk** – Avoid personal-use evictions near market-rent swings. Liability capped at 13 months' rent, but legal costs and RTB delays real. Stick to legitimate grounds (non-payment, lease breach, unit conversion).

5. **Inventory Watch – Affordable Segment** – 1–2 bedroom units remain tight (1–2% vacancy). Renew tenants early; keep quality tenants; price conservatively. This segment will hold value and cashflow.
